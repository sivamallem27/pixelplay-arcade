About PixelPlay Arcade

PixelPlay Arcade is a free collection of 35 original single-player browser games, made to be played instantly. There is nothing to download, nothing to install, and no account to create. Open the site on a phone, tablet or computer, tap a game, and play. Every game runs in the browser itself, so it works even on a modest device and a slow connection, and you can stop and come back later without losing your best scores.

The games were built as original implementations, not copies of other people's games. Classic ideas such as a snake that grows as it eats, a paddle that breaks bricks, or a maze with dots and ghosts are used as starting points, but the code, the artwork and the rules as they appear here were made for PixelPlay. No characters, music or artwork were taken from other games, and no game here asks you to pay, gamble, or win real money. Points and leaderboard positions are only for fun.

What you can play

The 35 games are grouped into five categories, so you can find the kind of game you are in the mood for without scrolling through everything.

Puzzle — think before you move

2048 Slide, Memory Match, Minesweeper Mini, Sliding Puzzle, Simon Pattern and Word Scramble Rush. These are quiet, turn-based games about memory, logic and planning. 2048 Slide asks you to slide numbered tiles together to reach 2048, Memory Match asks you to find matching pairs, Minesweeper Mini asks you to clear a small minefield without hitting a mine, Sliding Puzzle asks you to put numbered tiles back in order, Simon Pattern asks you to repeat a growing colour and sound sequence, and Word Scramble Rush asks you to unscramble words against the clock.

Classic Arcade — fast, simple, hard to put down

Snake, Breakout, Pong vs AI, Asteroid Shooter, Flappy Flyer, Pac Maze, Block Drop, Bubble Pop, Neon Trails, Bomb Runner and Checkers vs AI. These are the arcade-style games most players recognise at a glance: grow a snake without crashing, bounce a ball to break bricks, outscore a computer paddle, shoot asteroids, fly through gaps, eat dots while avoiding ghosts in a maze, stack falling blocks into complete lines, shoot coloured bubbles to match three, leave a light trail without crossing it, clear a bomb maze, or beat the computer at checkers.

Reflex & Speed — how fast are your hands?

Whack-a-Mole, Reaction Test, Aim Trainer, Stack Tower, Colour Rush and Fruit Slice Rush. These games are short and repeatable, built for quick sessions. Tap the moles before they disappear, react the instant a signal changes, hit targets as fast as you can, stack blocks without toppling the tower, match colours under time pressure, or slice falling fruit. They are the best games on the site for a two-minute break.

Board & Cards — play against the computer

Tic-Tac-Toe vs AI, Connect Four vs AI, Blackjack 21, Dice Duel, RPS League and Battleship vs AI. These are table games adapted for one player. You play against the computer, not against strangers, so there is no waiting for an opponent and no chat. Tic-Tac-Toe and Connect Four test whether you can outthink the computer's moves, Blackjack 21 tests whether you know when to hit or stand, Dice Duel and RPS League are quick luck-and-choice rounds, and Battleship vs AI tests whether you can find the computer's fleet before it finds yours.

Mini 3D & Racing — movement and dodging

3D Cube Runner, Car Dodge Racer, Ball Roll 3D, Space Blaster 3D, Endless Jump 3D and Drift Racer. These games use simple 3D-style graphics and movement instead of flat boards. Dodge obstacles while running, steer a car through traffic, roll a ball along a track, shoot enemies in space, jump across endless platforms, or drift around corners. They need a little more attention than the puzzle games, and they are a good test of timing and steering on both keyboard and touch.

How to play

Most games use the keyboard, the mouse, or touch, and the start screen of each game shows its own short description. As a general rule, arrow keys or WASD move a character, paddle or car, tapping or clicking selects, shoots or slices, and on a phone the on-screen buttons that appear for games such as Snake, Breakout, Pong and Cube Runner work the same way as the keyboard. The fullscreen button in the game view makes a game fill the whole screen, which helps on small phones. If a game feels too fast or too slow, that is part of its difficulty — there are no difficulty settings to change, and every player faces the same rules.

How scores and leaderboards work

Every game has its own leaderboard and its own best score. Your best scores and your play counts are saved only in your own browser, on your own device, using local storage. Nothing is sent to a server, because the site has no server-side user database and no accounts. That also means your scores do not follow you to another phone or computer, and clearing your browser data will clear them. Rival names shown on the leaderboards are pre-filled sample names so the boards never look empty — they are not other real players yet. A future update may add a global leaderboard; if it does, the Privacy Policy will be updated first to explain exactly what is stored.

Who PixelPlay is for

PixelPlay is for anyone who wants a quick game without an app store, a login screen, or a payment prompt. It suits school breaks, office breaks, parents looking for simple games for children, and players who enjoy beating their own best score rather than competing with strangers. The games contain no chat, no purchases, no gambling and no real-money prizes, and the site does not collect personal information. Parents and teachers can therefore let children play the puzzle and classic games without worrying about accounts or spending, although the site itself does not offer parental controls — supervision is up to the device owner.

How the site earns

PixelPlay Arcade is free for players. In future, advertising may be shown on the site to support it; any ad network used will be named in the Privacy Policy when ads go live. No ad code is running on the site yet, and players will never be asked to click ads. The two marked ad slots on the home page and the game-over screen are placeholders only until an approved network is added, and they are hidden until then.

What is next

The site is updated in small steps: fixing bugs players report, improving how games look and feel on phones, and adding new games when they are ready. Bug reports are welcome — the Contact page explains exactly what to include (the game name, what you tapped or pressed, and what happened) so a bug can be reproduced and fixed. Suggestions for new games are welcome too, as long as they can be built as original single-player browser games.
